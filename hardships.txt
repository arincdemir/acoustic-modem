Hardships & Lessons Learned
============================

1. Bit Detection via SNR Comparison
   Problem:  To decide whether a received symbol is bit-1 or bit-0, we check
             whether the signal is above the noise floor and then compare the
             SNR at freq1 vs. freq0 — whichever is higher wins.
   Gotcha:   We must verify that *both* frequencies are above the SNR
             threshold. Otherwise, any loud ambient sound (e.g. a high-pitched
             voice) that doesn't match freq1 would still be decoded as bit-0,
             since freq0's SNR would appear relatively higher by default.

2. Audio Buffer Truncation
   Problem:  Python's audio backend closes the output stream as soon as the
             last sample is written, which can cut off the final symbol.
   Solution: Append a short block of silence after the actual data so the
             stream stays open long enough for the last symbol to be fully
             played out.

3. Click / Pop Artifact Between Symbols ("Pfft" Sound)
   Problem:  Each new symbol started its sine wave at phase 0, regardless of
             where the previous symbol's waveform ended. This discontinuity
             caused an audible click (a "pfft" sound) at every symbol boundary.
   Solution: (a) Begin each new symbol at the ending phase of the previous one,
                 making the composite waveform continuous.
             (b) Apply a short amplitude fade-out at the end of each symbol to
                 further smooth the transition.
